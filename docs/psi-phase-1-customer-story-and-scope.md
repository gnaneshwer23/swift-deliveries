# DeliverX — Phase 1: Customer story and first pilot scope

24 October 2026 · Reviewable proposal grounded in founder direction and the current implementation audit. Website and commercial terms have not been changed.

## 1. The customer story

**DeliverX helps founders and product builders turn a product question into evidence, a decision they control, and work they can inspect.**

Specialist AI contributions share a product record so the reasons behind the work remain visible. The owner decides what moves forward. The long-term ambition is to connect those decisions to observed results and the next mission.

Public introduction to test:

> From product idea to continuous intelligence.
>
> Turn your product question into an evidence brief, compared options and a clear next step. Work with specialist AI contributions, keep the reasoning together, and approve the decisions that matter.

The existing headline can remain. Its supporting copy must describe concrete work before introducing the full future platform. Avoid implying that all catalogue roles or automatic learning are operational.

### Audience priority

| Priority | Person and situation | Useful first outcome |
|---|---|---|
| First pilot focus | A founder or individual builder with one product question and some supplied evidence | An inspectable decision pack and a scoped experiment brief. |
| Secondary | A product manager improving an existing workflow | Evidence, alternatives and rationale that can be shared with the team. |
| Same product, supported learning path | A student learning through a real product-building exercise | The same artefacts, with explanations of trade-offs and reflection prompts. |

This is a recommended focus for the first release, not a decision to exclude product teams or students. Student learning remains part of the vision. Avoid launching three separate products before one journey works reliably.

### The problem we own

The problem is the lost reasoning between research, decisions and delivery. A document moves forward, but its sources, assumptions, alternatives and acceptance criteria are left behind. DeliverX connects that record and makes handoffs inspectable.

The value proposition is not simply a large agent count. It is a useful answer with visible reasoning, accountable decisions and portable work.

## 2. First bounded pilot

**Recommended offer:** investigate one product question using supplied material, compare options, obtain an owner decision, and produce an inspectable decision pack and experiment brief.

This is a proposed delivery contract. It must pass Phase 4 runtime verification before it is promised as a working paid service.

| Item | Proposed scope |
|---|---|
| Customer brings | One product, target audience, question, constraints, desired outcome and authorised source material. |
| Intake | Validate that the question is answerable within scope; identify missing evidence before proceeding. |
| DeliverX contributes | Organised evidence and gaps; alternative approaches; recommendations and objections; a human decision point; a next-step artefact and export. |
| Customer controls | Evidence supplied, scope, decision, any consequential action and any additional usage. |
| End state | A completed or explicitly inconclusive decision pack. Missing evidence may justify a research plan rather than a confident recommendation. |
| Excluded from this first promise | Customer interviews conducted by agents, production code changes, deployment, external communications, automatic analytics collection and autonomous learning. |

### What the customer receives

1. **Mission brief:** question, audience, intended outcome, constraints and acceptance criteria.
2. **Evidence register:** supplied references, supported observations, assumptions and unresolved gaps.
3. **Options and decision record:** alternatives, trade-offs, recommendation, objections and the owner's recorded choice or request for more evidence.
4. **Experiment brief:** proposed change, what would be measured, baseline requirement, observation window and review question. Expected results are hypotheses.
5. **Portable export:** these records, source references and provenance labels describing model-generated, deterministic, simulated and human-authored contributions.

A lightweight prototype is an optional agreed extension after the selected path is verified. It is not equivalent to tested or deployed customer software. Avoid making a prototype mandatory for every kind of mission.

### Acceptance of a pilot mission

- The customer can trace each material recommendation to supplied evidence or an explicitly labelled assumption.
- Alternatives and dissent remain inspectable after the decision.
- A person approves the specific next scope; the demonstration never stands in for real approval.
- The record survives reload and interruption, and can be exported.
- Missing permission, evidence or budget produces a visible pause or inconclusive result rather than fabricated completion.
- No claim of product improvement is made until an observed result exists.

## 3. The single example story

**Sample product:** an early-stage product with an account setup journey.

**Mission question:** “Which onboarding change should we investigate first?”

Use this across Home, Product and How it works. Do not invent customer interviews, baseline figures or success percentages. Example notes must be labelled hypothetical; a real pilot uses authorised supplied evidence.

| Step | Sender → recipient | What moves | Visitor interaction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frame | Owner → Problem Framing | Question, constraints, intended outcome | Inspect the sample brief and unanswered questions. |
| Investigate | Research Synthesis → Product Discovery | Evidence brief with references and gaps | Open a sample observation and its source label. |
| Compare | Product Strategy → scoped reviewers | Options and trade-offs | Compare options and inspect objections. |
| Decide | Specialist recommendation → owner | Decision pack, risks and uncertainty | Approve an example scope or request more evidence; explicitly no workspace mutation. |
| Prepare | Approved scope → relevant specialist contribution | Experiment brief and acceptance criteria | Inspect/download a labelled sample output. |
| Preserve | Mission record → Product Brain | Evidence, decision and artefact relationships | Follow why an output exists and who decided. |
| Learn, later capability | Supplied observed result → outcome review → owner | Comparison and proposed next mission | See a clearly labelled planned preview until Phase 5 is implemented and verified. |

These are explanatory role contributions, not a promise that every named role currently executes. Runtime capability selection must follow the verified capability table.

PayNudge can later become a separate example selected explicitly, rather than appearing midway through the onboarding story.

### Labels and vocabulary

Use **Interactive example**, **Planned**, and **Implemented foundation — release verification pending** where appropriate. Use **Evaluated in preview** only with retained evidence, scope and date; it does not mean generally available in production.

Output assurance remains the repository's specific labels: **Agent-reviewed / Automatically tested / Human-reviewed / Externally attested**. Do not collapse them into a generic “Verified.” Record generation mode separately from review state. Agent agreement or weighted support is not factual accuracy, confidence calibration or independent evidence.

Keep detailed implementation caveats in an accessible status panel while placing the key limitation beside the relevant claim or action. Disclaimers must not contradict a stronger headline.

## 6. Commercial scope to carry forward

Keep the existing Builder £29, Founder £99 and Studio £299 monthly figures as pilot-offer hypotheses, with availability and scope confirmed before paid activation. No price change is proposed in Phase 1. This document does not validate margins or willingness to pay.

The current seat/product/mission quotas and model allowances are not enforced paid entitlements. Confirm actual support capacity, collaboration behaviour and provider costs before committing to them. “First product launch” language should become “first product decision” for the bounded pilot until delivery capability supports launch claims.

Creating an account and activating an agreed paid pilot are different events. Define that distinction clearly; remove the current implication that the whole service is free during early access. A future checkout must not silently activate capabilities that have not passed release checks.
